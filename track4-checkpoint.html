Track 4 Checkpoint: Can You Reason About RNA?

Ten new questions you have not seen in the lessons. Each one joins RNA structure, protein binding and modifications, the way real RNA-biology papers do. Take it at the end of Track 4 to find out what you already own and what to review.

Track checkpoint ~20 min In your browser, no code

What this checks

  • You can connect RNA structure with whether a protein can bind.
  • You can read CLIP data for real binding rather than abundance.
  • You can judge whether m6A evidence is convincing.
  • You can recognise other RNA changes, such as A-to-I editing, in sequencing data.

Before you start

  • Finish the Track 4 lessons on the roadmap. This is the advanced track, so expect harder questions.
  • No notes on the first try. The point is to find out what you can recall, which is also one of the best ways to make it stick.

How this checkpoint works

Answer all ten questions. When you finish, the page shows your score. 7 of 10 counts as a pass; aim for 8 or more before moving on. Every explanation links to the lesson to review.

This is a self-check, not an exam. The answers live in the page, so it only works if you answer honestly before reading the explanation. If you score below 7, review the linked lessons, wait a day or two, then reload and try again. Coming back after a break helps you remember more than re-reading straight away.

The ten questions

Scenario questions. Pick the best answer; some wrong answers are tempting on purpose.

A known RNA-binding protein motif sits in a region that in-cell SHAPE data shows as strongly base-paired (low reactivity). CLIP finds almost no binding there. What is the most likely explanation?
A sequence motif is necessary but not enough: the RNA must also be accessible. Low SHAPE reactivity means constrained, usually paired, nucleotides. Structure and binding have to be read together (Spitale et al. 2015). Review: RNA structure and CLIP-seq.
hnRNPC is not a classic m6A reader, yet its binding at some sites rises when a nearby adenosine is methylated. What explains this?
m6A weakens base pairing. In the m6A switch model, this structural change exposes a nearby binding site, so m6A can affect proteins that never touch it directly (Liu et al. 2015). Review: RNA structure.
In iCLIP or eCLIP data, why do analysts focus on where each read starts rather than on the full read coverage?
The short peptide left on the RNA after protease digestion blocks reverse transcription. Truncated cDNAs therefore begin right next to the crosslink site, which is what gives iCLIP its high resolution (König et al. 2010). In paired-end eCLIP, use the start of read 2. Review: CLIP-seq.
Gene A has 10 times more CLIP reads than gene B, but gene A is also about 10 times more highly expressed. Does the RBP prefer gene A?
Abundant RNAs produce more reads in any library. A size-matched input shows the background each region would give anyway, and enrichment over it is what reflects binding (Van Nostrand et al. 2016). Review: CLIP-seq.
Which result best supports that your MeRIP-seq peaks on mRNAs reflect real m6A rather than antibody artefacts?
METTL3 (with METTL14) installs most internal m6A in mRNA, so real mRNA peaks should weaken when it is removed. Real m6A peaks also show the expected motif and enrichment near stop codons (Dominissini et al. 2012). Repeating the same antibody only shows the artefact is reproducible. Review: Analysing m6A-seq and Epitranscriptomics 101.
You want the structure of a 300-nucleotide region inside a 6,000-nucleotide mRNA. Which approach is most sensible?
For mRNAs, local folding predicts structure more accurately than folding the whole molecule at once (Lange et al. 2012), and in cells, proteins and folding during transcription also shape structure. Local folding tools in ViennaRNA and probing constraints give more reliable, testable models (Lorenz et al. 2011). Review: RNA structure.
In RNA-seq aligned to the genome, one site shows A→G mismatches in many reads, but DNA sequencing of the same person shows only A at that site. What is the most likely cause?
ADAR enzymes convert adenosine to inosine in RNA. Reverse transcriptase pairs inosine with C, so the sequenced base appears as G. Because the DNA is unchanged, the difference is in the RNA itself (Eisenberg and Levanon 2018). On genes on the minus strand it appears as T→C against the genome. m6A does not normally change the base read. Review: Epitranscriptomics 101.
CLIP peaks for a nuclear splicing factor fall mostly in introns, while peaks for a cytoplasmic RBP fall mostly in 3' UTRs. What should you conclude?
Where peaks fall is a useful sanity check. The first HITS-CLIP study found the splicing regulator Nova bound near the alternative exons it controls, largely in introns (Licatalosi et al. 2008). Review: CLIP-seq.
You deplete YTHDF2 alone and see only a small change in the stability of m6A-marked mRNAs. Which explanation fits one current, well-supported model?
The "unified model" proposes that the three YTHDF paralogs share function and together mediate decay, so removing one gives a modest effect (Zaccara and Jaffrey 2020). This is still debated, which is why you would test the paralogs together. Review: Integrative thinking and Epitranscriptomics 101.
Which eCLIP peak set should you trust most for follow-up?
Enrichment over input removes abundance-driven background, and agreement between replicates guards against chance. ENCODE eCLIP analyses require both (Van Nostrand et al. 2016; 2020). Review: CLIP-seq.
